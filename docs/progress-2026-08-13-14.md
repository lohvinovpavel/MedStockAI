## Shipped

### COMP-1 — certification traffic light
Every stocked drug gets a colour derived from FDA records: red for an expired
listing, a withdrawn application or an ongoing Class I recall; yellow for expiry
within 90 days, a Class II/III recall, an unapproved marketing category, or an
FDA shortage.

The colour is derived, never authored. Rules and thresholds live in one file so
there is one place to argue with them. `GET /status` (batch, 100 NDCs),
`GET /certificates/{ndc}`, `GET /ruleset`. Inventory page shows the badge; if
`compliance` is down the shelf still renders and badges go grey.

### COMP-2 — on-demand exploration of unknown drugs
An NDC with no certification row is resolved at the moment someone looks. The
obvious approach does not work — openFDA's directory lists only currently
marketed products, so re-querying it answers nothing. Measured against 18 real
unknowns: the directory resolved 0, the label endpoint 3, **RxNorm's NDC status
endpoint 18**. One of those has been obsolete since 2012 and now renders red,
which the scheduled feed could never have produced.

### PP-1 / PP-2 — patient assessment and cohort purchasing
Deterministic pipeline: validate, normalise, expand, hard gates, interactions,
organ function, age rules, aggregate. Hard gates short-circuit and produce
**no score** — a number beside an absolute contraindication invites someone to
weigh it against a discount.

`POST /demand` turns a cohort into a purchasing plan. When COMP-1 marks a drug
unavailable, each patient is re-routed to the safest **in-class** alternative
for them individually; patients with no tolerated alternative are counted with a
reason rather than dropped.

### PP-3 — AI prognosis from label prose
The model predicts the population, not the person. It reads FDA label text and
extracts which patient characteristics raise the risk of which reaction; the
per-patient answer is arithmetic against that table.

Four gates: a closed vocabulary (a model inventing `smoking_status` is rejected
mechanically), a verbatim citation check, pharmacist approval before anything
colours a screen, and the `spl_id` so an old prediction can still be explained.

Real output: amoxicillin → anaphylaxis conditioned on a prior penicillin
reaction, and rash conditioned on ICD-10 B27 (*"A high percentage of patients
with mononucleosis who receive amoxicillin develop an erythematous skin rash"*).
Lisinopril → acute renal failure conditioned on renal artery stenosis, CKD and
heart failure. Nine profiles, 9/9 structurally valid, all `awaiting_approval`.

## Found by measuring, not by reasoning

**A listing-expiry rule would have turned 70% of a formulary amber every
October.** 70.5% of products share one annual expiry date. Only a two-year
time-travel simulation caught it; the rule now fires on lapsed listings only.

**One rule was 99% of the amber.** 372 of 375 flagged products were
`UNAPPROVED_CATEGORY`, a permanent attribute. Splitting findings into transient
events and standing properties cut the alert surface from 375 to 5.

**Red barely fires from the directory alone.** 0.0% of 3,000 real products.
Recalls and RxNorm obsolescence are where red actually comes from.

**61% of ongoing recalls cannot be joined to a drug.** A regex tops out at 40%
and mistakes ZIP+4 codes and UPCs for NDCs — it would put a red badge on an
unrelated drug. This is the measured case for the `extract` task.
